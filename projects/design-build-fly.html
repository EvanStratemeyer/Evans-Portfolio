Build Fly

An AIAA-sanctioned intercollegiate competition to design, build, and fly a radio-controlled aircraft optimized for a specific mission profile.

✈   Contributed to a competitive AIAA aircraft build — designing structural and mechanical subsystems for a multi-component autonomous drone + carrier plane configuration.

Overview

The Design Build Fly (DBF) competition, hosted by the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics, challenges university teams to design and build radio-controlled aircraft that complete specific mission tasks. Our team at Boston University tackled a configuration involving a larger carrier plane and an autonomous drone payload.

My contributions focused on the structural and mechanical integration of key subsystems — ensuring components were manufacturable, within weight budget, and mechanically sound under flight loads.

Battery Housing Design

Designed the battery housing for the aircraft in a collaborative Onshape file shared across the team. The housing needed to securely retain the battery pack under flight loads and vibration while remaining lightweight and accessible for quick swaps between missions.

Latch Mechanism Mount

Designed a mounting plate for the latch mechanism that links the autonomous drone to the larger carrier plane. This interface needed to handle the dynamic loads of release while maintaining a secure connection during taxi, takeoff, and flight — a genuine mechanical engineering challenge with real safety implications.

Wing Assembly

Assembled 3D-printed wing sections using carbon fiber rods as structural spars, providing stiffness and load-carrying capability without significant weight penalty. Integrated servo motors into the wing assembly to actuate control surfaces (flaps), allowing active aerodynamic control during flight.
