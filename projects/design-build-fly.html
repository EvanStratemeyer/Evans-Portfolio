An AIAA-sanctioned intercollegiate competition to design, build, and fly a radio-controlled aircraft optimized for a specific mission profile.

✈   Contributed to a competitive AIAA aircraft build — designing structural and mechanical subsystems for a multi-component autonomous drone + carrier plane configuration.

Overview

DBF is an AIAA intercollegiate competition where teams design and fly radio-controlled aircraft around a specific mission profile. Our BU team built a carrier plane that deployed an autonomous drone — which made the mechanical integration a lot more complex than a standard RC build.

I worked on the structural and mechanical subsystems, focused on keeping everything manufacturable and within weight budget.

Battery Housing Design

I designed the battery housing in our shared Onshape file. It needed to hold the pack securely through takeoff and flight while still being quick to swap between missions — so the access and retention geometry took a few iterations to get right.

Latch Mechanism Mount

I also designed the mounting plate for the latch that connects the autonomous drone to the carrier plane. The interface has to hold securely through taxi, takeoff, and flight, then release cleanly on cue — getting the load paths and release geometry right took a lot of back-and-forth.

Wing Assembly

For the wings, I assembled 3D-printed sections onto carbon fiber rod spars, then integrated servo motors to drive the flaps. Getting the servos seated and the linkages moving without binding took more fitting work than I expected.
