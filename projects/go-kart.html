Sep 2023 — Apr 2024 Personal Project

Go-Kart

Designed and built a functional go-kart capable of 30 mph, keeping total weight under 200 lbs and total cost under $1,000.

🏎   30 mph top speed · Under 200 lbs · Under $1,000 budget · Sourced from 5+ suppliers — all targets hit.

Overview

I spent most of junior year designing and building a go-kart with a friend. The goal was simple: hit 30 mph, keep it under 200 lbs, and spend less than $1,000. I handled all the CAD and research; we built the frame together.

Design & Modeling

Before ordering anything or cutting metal, I modeled the whole kart in Onshape — frame, steering, and drive system in one assembly. Having it all in CAD let me catch clearance problems early and build a cut list directly from the model.

I also brought the frame into SolidWorks to run structural simulations and check the steering geometry. The Ackermann angle was something I spent a lot of time on to make sure the kart would turn right at low speed.

Sourcing & Budget

Sourcing parts from 5+ vendors while staying under $1,000 meant making a lot of calls between cost and quality. I kept a spreadsheet tracking every part, price, lead time, and status — when something was backordered or too expensive, I had to find an alternative fast.

Build & Testing

I learned MIG welding to build the frame. I practiced on scrap steel first until the welds looked right, then we tack-welded the full frame to check alignment before running full welds on every joint. Once the engine was on and everything was assembled, we got it up to 30 mph.
